# BTAM Analyzer Reader Knowledge Guide

## Core Standard

Assessment is not a score. Assessment is the process of turning concern into clarity, clarity into action, and action into monitored support.

## Professional End-User Flow

1. Identify the concern.
2. Separate verified facts from opinions or assumptions.
3. Identify behavior themes.
4. Identify missing facts.
5. Decide what must happen today.
6. Identify supportive intervention options.
7. Assign an owner.
8. Set a follow-up date.
9. Require human BTAM team review.
10. Document the proof-of-care trail.

## Interpretation Levels

Routine means there is not enough concerning information yet, but the concern should still be documented and monitored.

Low means there is a concern, but it appears stable and manageable with support and monitoring.

Moderate means the team should review promptly because multiple concern themes or stressors are present.

High means same-day review is needed because threat, target, escalation, planning, or access concerns may be present.

Critical means normal workflow stops and emergency/safety procedures should be followed according to local policy.

## Accuracy Rules

- Use observable facts.
- Do not label the person.
- Do not assume intent.
- Do not treat the tool output as the final answer.
- Do not close the case without a follow-up owner and date.
- Do not rely on discipline alone when support is needed.
- Do not ignore missing facts.

## Proof-of-Care Logic

Concern received → facts organized → themes identified → unknowns listed → action assigned → support considered → follow-up scheduled → human BTAM team review required.
